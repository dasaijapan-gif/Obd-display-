# Mounting and safety

## Where to put it

- **On the bike, not on you.** The lean is measured from the toy's own sensor, so it
  must turn with the bike. A tank, top triple clamp (yoke) or handlebar mount works.
- **Rigid beats soft.** A wobbly mount adds its own swinging to the lean reading. Keep
  the arm short. A thin rubber damper between case and mount is fine; a long flexible
  arm isn't.
- **Screen facing you.** The firmware works out "forward" from which way the screen
  faces, and it is meant to be glanced at.
- **Clear of the steering lock, cables, mirrors and your hands**, and nothing in front
  of the instruments or lights.

Handlebar mounts turn with the steering, but at speed the bars only turn a few degrees,
so the lean reading is barely affected.

## First time

1. Serial monitor (115200 baud) → send `o` → follow the orientation wizard on screen:
   flat on a table screen-up, then upright facing you.
2. Mount it. Hold the bike upright (off the side stand) and still until it says READY.
3. Lean the bike a little to the right: the gauge round the rim should fill clockwise.

To recalibrate later, hold the bike upright and hold the BOOT button (or touch the
screen) for 2 seconds.

## Safety

- **It is a toy, not an instrument.** The lean angle and G-forces are estimates for fun.
  Don't use them to judge how much grip you have.
- **Don't look at it in a corner.** The design helps: while the bike is leaned over
  nothing on screen needs reading, and corner reports and records wait until you're
  upright again. Still, your eyes belong on the road.
- **Secure it properly.** Something coming loose at speed is dangerous to you and to
  others. Check the mount before each ride, and use a lanyard or tether on the case.
- **Battery.** LiPo cells don't like heat: don't leave it on a black tank in full sun,
  and use a protected cell. Unplug or switch it off when the bike is parked for long.
- **Local rules.** Some places restrict screens and objects mounted on handlebars.
